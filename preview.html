🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

Let's work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer. No background needed. Each idea is one sentence, one example, one number from the tests.

Step 1 — What is a stock? (own a slice)

A stock is a slice of a company. Common stock votes and may pay a dividend (cash share of profit); preferred usually pays a fixed dividend first but does not vote. A portfolio is all your slices together. Only listed (public) companies trade on an exchange (e.g. NYSE) via a broker who charges a commission. Each stock has a ticker (AAPL, TSLA). An index like the S&P 500 bundles 500 giants as the benchmark — outperforming means beating it. Volume/liquidity = how easily you can buy/sell without moving the price (Apple = high; tiny stocks = low).

Proof link: our basket holds AAPL/MSFT/NVDA/TSLA/KO against SPY — see dashboard.

Step 2 — Is the company healthy? (5 numbers)

Market cap = price × shares. Enterprise value adds debt, subtracts cash (the full takeover bill). Revenue is sales; net profit is what remains. EPS = profit per share; P/E = price ÷ EPS (high = expensive). PEG adjusts for growth. Dividend yield = yearly dividend ÷ price. Free cash flow = cash left for dividends/buybacks/growth. Margins (gross → operating → net) show profit per $1 of sales.

Example verified: Apple 2025 revenue $416.2B (+6.4%), net margin 26.9%; live P/Es — TSLA 353.1 (priced for perfection) vs KO 26.1 (steady).

Step 3 — What flavor? (growth, value, defensive)

Growth reinvests and races (NVDA); value looks cheap vs fundamentals; blue-chip is a trusted giant (Coca-Cola); cyclical swings with the economy (cars, airlines); defensive sells what people always buy (food, utilities, healthcare). Size: large >$10B, mid $2–10B, small <$2B, penny <$5 (lottery tickets — usually a bad idea).

Step 4 — What else can I buy? (funds, gold, bonds, FX, crypto)

Mutual fund = pooled basket with a manager; ETF = basket trading live like a stock; index fund copies an index (the S&P 500 ETF is the default); hedge fund uses fancy/risky tricks for the wealthy. Also: commodities (gold/oil), bonds (lend, earn interest, get principal at maturity), currencies (dollar/euro/yen), crypto (Bitcoin/Ethereum — volatile, equity-linked here: corr 0.41).

Step 5 — Why do prices jump? (mood + regimes)

Volatility = bumpiness; VIX = expected next-30-day bumpiness (the "fear index"). Bull = long rise (optimism); bear = long fall (fear). Correction = −10%; rally = sharp bounce; crash = sudden plunge; bubble = hype that pops. Sentiment often moves prices more than fundamentals short-term.

Step 6 — How should I invest? (the 3 answers that survived testing)

Passive (buy the whole market, hold, keep fees low) vs active (pick + trade a lot — usually loses after fees). Invest everything now (lump-sum) beats drip-feeding (DCA) most of the time (81.2% here) because markets rise ~7 years in 10 — waiting in cash costs you. Buy-and-hold + compounding (gains earn gains) beats buy-the-dip timing (−21.8% gap) and market timing/speculation. DCA is still fine as a habit from each paycheck, or as courage-training for nervous beginners (6 months max). Fundamental = study the business; technical = study charts/volume; macro = study rates/inflation/growth.

Step 7 — Company events & paydays

IPO = first public sale; secondary offering = more shares later; buyback = company buys its own shares (fewer left, each worth more); split 2-for-1 = twice the shares at half price (same pie); reverse split = the opposite; acquisition = one company buys another (target usually jumps). Ex-div date = must own before this to get the dividend; pay date = cash arrives. Insider trading on secret info = crime.

Step 8 — Don't lose it all (risk in one page)

Diversify (spread across stocks/sectors/assets), allocate (split stocks/bonds/cash to your goal), rebalance (reset percentages yearly). Alpha = beating the market; beta = moving with the market (1.0 = same, 0.65 = calmer); Sharpe = return per unit of risk (higher = better sleep); correlation = do two assets zig together (gold 0.16 = good diversifier). Systematic risk (recession, rates, politics) hits all; unsystematic (bad CEO, failed product) can be diversified away.

Proof: 60/40 SPY/gold beta 0.67, Sharpe 0.86, worst loss −18.7% vs −24.5% for all-stocks.

Step 9 — The economy behind it (60 seconds)

Inflation = money buys less; deflation = prices fall (people wait, economy stalls); stagflation = high prices + no jobs (painful). Monetary policy (central bank): cut rates → stimulate; raise → cool down. Fiscal policy (government): spend/build → expand; cut back → austerity.

🔬 Methods (one paragraph each)

  • Total-return prices (split/dividend-adjusted, repaired) — gains include dividends, not just price.
  • No peeking: dip signals use only past 20 days, invested next day; drip-feed tranches compound forward only.
  • Cash earns 4.5% while waiting (today's savings/T-bill reality); fees/taxes omitted (this favors stock-picking, so index wins are conservative).
  • Survivorship disclosed: we test famous survivors — long-run inference uses the survivorship-corrected 20-year base rate (92.6% of pros trail).
  • Risk math: CAGR (yearly growth), volatility, Sharpe (return per risk), max drawdown (worst fall), beta (market sensitivity), alpha (excess), correlation (move together?).
